Was it truly enough?

July 1, 2026

I was introduced to the idea of stoicism sometime during my freshman year of college, and aside from a few disagreements I had with it, I thought it was a great mindset to have. Stoicism is a philosophy that teaches mastering your emotions/reactions, and focusing only on what you can control. A principle we can derive from this is to work as hard as you can toward your goals while accepting that the outcome is ultimately beyond your control. A similar idea exists in Islam: make every effort, then place your trust in God.

For the past few months though, I’ve been conflicted: what is “hard enough”? How do you know you gave it your all? For example, I’ve been extremely fortunate over the years to interview with many tech companies I would’ve loved to work at, but most ending with unfortunate rejections. So many final rounds, so many close calls, so many “almosts”. I’ve learned a lot from every single one, but sometimes I question: did I really try my hardest when preparing? What if instead of that one hour break on Netflix, I solved an extra leetcode problem that might’ve shown up on the interview? Or instead of going to the gym for an hour, I had cut it in half and used the rest of the time to revise rate limiting strategies? In this case, wouldn’t “trying your hardest” theoretically mean studying and improving every waking minute, or as close to that as possible?

Thinking about this for a long time, I realized my perception of “hard work” was wrong, in that I was viewing rest and breaks as a disadvantage, when in reality they're just as essential as the work itself. Additionally, hard work is not the only thing that matters, and it’s not the only thing you have control of. Having a solid plan for what you want to accomplish, consistently executing it over a long period of time, and focusing on the right things are also extremely important, are well within your control AND just as important.

Could we do all these things perfectly? Probably not, we’re humans and flawed. My friend brought up a great point while discussing this: hindsight is 20/20. Looking back, it’s easy to convince yourself you could have studied another hour, worked a little harder, or made a better decision. But those judgments are made with information you didn’t have in the moment.

What matters is your effort in that moment, not what you think it should have been.

Did you genuinely focus on your task? Did you rest because you were tired, or as an excuse to avoid the work? Was your plan solid and realistic? Those are also important questions to ask when reflecting, instead of dwelling purely on whether your work output was 100%.

"A bow that is always bent will soon break."
